01 — The Handi

A desi kitchen
named for its pot.

Handi House sits on GT Road in Jada, Jhelum — the stretch of road where travellers stop and families gather. The house is named for the handi: the heavy, round-bottomed pot that a curry is cooked in, sealed and slow, until the meat gives and the gravy turns deep.

That is the whole idea here. No shortcuts in the pot, breads baked to order, and a table where a family can sit down together after a long drive or a long day.

I

The karahi

Chicken and mutton karahi, cooked in the wok over a hard flame and finished with ginger and green chilli.

II

The tandoor

Naan, roti and paratha baked in the clay oven and brought to the table while they are still steaming.

III

The table

Platters and handis made to share — the kind of meal where everyone reaches for the same dish.

From the spice box

First the spices.
Then the pot.

A handi is only as good as what goes into it. The spice box is the start of every dish here — red chilli, turmeric, cumin and coriander, ground and measured by hand, before the pot ever meets the flame.

  1. 01 — Order at the table. Pick the handi, the bread and the rice; the kitchen starts from scratch.
  2. 02 — Cooked in the handi. Slow, sealed and stirred, until the gravy thickens and the meat softens.
  3. 03 — Served with hot naan. The bread comes off the tandoor as the handi reaches the table.

03 — Breads & Rice

Bread off the tandoor.
Rice from the pot.

A handi without bread is unfinished. The tandoor runs all day, and the rice pots come off the flame to order.

From the Tawa

Parathas cooked on the flat griddle, layered and crisp at the edges — the bread that stands up to a thick handi gravy.

From the Tandoor

Naan and roti slapped onto the clay oven wall and pulled off blistered and hot. Garlic naan for the karahi, plain roti for everything else.

From the Rice Pot

Chicken biryani layered in the pot with saffron and fried onion, served with raita — the rice dish the table orders when the whole family is out.
